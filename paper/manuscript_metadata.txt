ABSTRACT (250 words)
================================================================================

We introduce a novel task for evaluating sentence encoders: detecting plot-level semantic similarity in full-length literary texts, where surface-level lexical and stylistic features are deliberately varied. Using Robinson Crusoe adaptations (1719-1903) as a case study, we construct a balanced binary classification dataset of 2,968 texts where positive examples share core narrative structures (shipwreck, isolation, survival, self-reliance) but differ in vocabulary, style, setting, and character names. This task tests whether embeddings capture compositional semantics beyond word-level similarity. We systematically evaluate 15 embedding models across three architectural paradigms: bi-encoders (USE, SBERT, E5, BGE), cross-encoders (BERT, RoBERTa), and encoder-decoder models (T5, FLAN-T5). Our bi-encoder baseline (Universal Sentence Encoder + neural classifier) achieves 99.3% accuracy with 0.18s inference time. We find that: (1) modern embeddings (E5, BGE) do not outperform USE for this task despite superior performance on standard benchmarks, (2) cross-encoders provide only 0.2% accuracy gain at 48× computational cost, (3) zero-shot LLMs achieve 76-91% accuracy, far below supervised baselines, and (4) architectural choice determines feasibility of corpus-scale analysis (bi-encoders: 39 days for 17M texts; cross-encoders: 4.7 years). We release our dataset, trained models, and evaluation code as a benchmark for long-document semantic similarity.
